# Discovery Questions

The dossier gives Ben questions to ask on the call. Good discovery questions do double duty: they build rapport AND they surface (and size) the pains that justify a build. Assemble three things: **Ben's staples**, a **tailored set** for this prospect, and **one validating question per opportunity**. Put the 3 strongest at the very top for live glancing.

Each question in the dossier should carry a short "why / listen for" hint, so mid-call Ben knows what he's fishing for (e.g. *listen for the manual callback loop*).

## Ben's staples (almost always include)

- **"Walk me through a normal day or week, where does your time actually go?"** The open-ended workhorse. Listen for the repetitive manual loops and the task they dread.
- **"What CRM and back-office systems do you run day to day?"** Confirms the incumbent stack and the integration surface. Listen for how many systems and whether they talk to each other.
- **"Have you used AI before, what have you tried, and how did it go?"** Gauges AI maturity and surfaces objections. Listen for "tried ChatGPT but it craps out / I don't trust it with client data / it sounds like a robot", each sets up a custom or compliant angle.

## Tailored categories (pick what this prospect's situation calls for)

**The work itself**
- "What's the single most repetitive task you or your team do, the one you could do in your sleep?"
- "What's the task you put off until the end of the day or the end of the season?" (avoidance = pain)
- "If you cloned yourself, what's the first thing you'd hand the clone?" (surfaces the bottleneck fast)

**People and bottleneck**
- "Who touches this process besides you?" (stakeholders + where it stalls)
- "Are you weighing any hires right now? For what?" (the cheaper-than-headcount frame; an agency on the fence about a $20/hr admin is a live opportunity)
- "What only happens when you personally do it?" (key-person risk)

**Where things break**
- "Walk me through the last time something fell through the cracks, what happened?" (stakes and cost; a missed item that cost a client real money is the sharpest motivator)
- "What do clients complain about, or what do you wish you were faster at?"

**Data and compliance** (regulated verticals: insurance/health, CPA/tax, advisory/SEC, legal)
- "What kind of client data are you handling, and what can't go into a tool like ChatGPT today?" (surfaces the compliance gate, often the highest-value, currently-untouchable task)

**Systems and data flow**
- "When the same client info needs to live in two systems, how does it get there?" (re-entry pain)
- "How do you keep track of who you owe what, and by when?" (task/open-loop tracking)

**Growth, goals, and buying context**
- "If you got 5-10 hours a week back, what would you spend them on?" (ties ROI to their ambition, and reveals what they value)
- "What's capping your growth right now?"
- "What would have to be true for a tool like this to be a no-brainer for you?" (buying criteria)
- "What does a great outcome look like 90 days from now?" (success metric, and a soft close)
- "Who else would weigh in on a decision like this?" (decision process)

## Per-opportunity validating questions

For each opportunity from Step 3, generate one question that both confirms the pain is real and helps **size** it. The pattern: turn the opportunity into a "how do you handle X today / how long does X take" question.
- Re-quote engine -> "When a policy comes up for renewal or you remarket a cancellation, walk me through what that takes today, how many carriers, how long per client?"
- Bank-vs-QBO reconciliation -> "How do you reconcile the bank statements against QuickBooks right now, and how often does something slip through?"
- Document-chasing nag -> "When you're waiting on documents from a client, how do you chase them, and how much of the season goes to that?"
- Keep-in-touch -> "How do you stay in front of the [referral sources / clients / donors] who drive your business today?"
- Daily briefing -> "How do you figure out who to focus on each morning?"

These are the questions that convert a hypothesis into a sized, real opportunity on the call.

## Convert unknowns into questions

Anything the research left as **Unknown** should become a question rather than a guess. If you couldn't confirm their CRM, ask it. If you couldn't tell whether they're the decision-maker, ask it. This is why "we don't know" is useful, not a failure: every gap is a reason to ask, and asking is what discovery is for.

## Live-use ordering

The top 3 questions (surfaced in the cheat-sheet) should be: one warm opener tied to a fun fact or their business, one that opens the biggest suspected pain, and one that sizes the hero opportunity. The rest live in the full question bank below the fold.
